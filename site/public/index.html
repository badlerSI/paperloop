Reel 1 · The tsunami

A wave was always coming for ed tech.

For years, cash-strapped districts paid for software that mostly sat unused and barely moved test scores, while reading fell. That business was never going to last.

I'm a teacher. I built Paper Loop in three days, as a side project, using under $50 of a monthly Claude plan. It does what the subscriptions promised: a specific comment on every answer, for every student, on paper, by morning. It's free.

Reel 2 · The receipts

Districts paid. Students didn't gain.

The decline started years before the pandemic. The money that followed it bought less than anyone hoped, and the software it bought mostly went unused.

2013

The year US reading and math scores started falling, seven years before schools closed for COVID.1

6 days

Extra learning bought by each $1,000 per student of federal relief money, 2022–2025. That's about what any general budget increase buys. Schools received about $190 billion.1

Reel 3 · Why it was bound to happen

What works was always cheap. The expensive part was a teacher's time.

The strongest results in education research come from cheap, human things: students practicing what they learned, specific comments on their work, and parents told the truth. None of it needs a license. It needs someone to read every answer, and AI just made that nearly free.

g = 0.50

Practice that makes students recall

Classroom quizzing, across 222 studies. A short worksheet the next day is retrieval practice.7

d = 0.48

Specific feedback

Feedback that says what to fix does about twice as well as the average. A grade beside a comment cancels the comment, so comments alone work best.89

+0.18

Marked homework, with the common wrong answers

A homework tool that raised scores in an independent 43-school trial works by marking every problem and showing the teacher which mistakes were common. Paper Loop does the same on paper.10

54 studies

Reading on paper

Readers understand informational text better on paper than on screens, and the gap is growing.11

27% fewer

Telling parents the truth

Weekly texts to parents about missed work, grades and absences cut course failures by 27%. 32,000 texts cost $63.12

1st

Mississippi's reading law

Screening every young reader, coaches in classrooms, a reading plan for each struggling student, a note to their parents, and a third-grade gate. After adjusting for student demographics, Mississippi now ranks first in 4th-grade reading.1314

When the expensive part becomes nearly free, the middlemen go.

A teacher with 150 students and a six-question worksheet has 900 answers to read. That's why the best practices never scaled, and why districts bought software that promised to do it instead. Claude can now draft every one of those comments overnight, with the teacher checking. A subscription has nothing left to sell.

Reel 5 · The weapon

A copier, a stack of paper, and Claude.

Paper Loop is free and open source. It runs inside Claude for Teachers, which is free for verified US K-12 teachers. Students never touch a screen.

  1. 1

    Plan

    Claude plans the lesson and makes the worksheet, with versions for students who need them.

  2. 2

    Print

    On any copier. Students write a class ID number in the boxes, never their name.

  3. 3

    Write

    Students work on paper: no logins, no tabs, no notifications.

  4. 4

    Scan

    The teacher scans the whole stack on the school copier.

  5. 5

    Mark

    Claude reads every answer against the key and writes a comment beside it. Anything it isn't sure of goes to the teacher first.

  6. 6

    Hand back

    Marked pages go back the next day, with go-over slides, a practice sheet for each student, and a page for families.

…and the results plan tomorrow's lesson.

Reel 6 · We the teachers

We are the many. Ed tech is the few.

There are 3.8 million public school teachers in this country.15 We buy the supplies our budgets don't cover. We crowdfund the books our students keep.16 We give up our evenings to the marking. Meanwhile our districts renew subscriptions that mostly go unopened.2

Ed tech isn't our partner. It's the enemy of every budget line that should have paid for a teacher, a tutor, or a book. Squeezing cash-strapped districts for profit is over.

Fight capitalism with capitalism.

A better product that costs less beats a worse one every time, even one that calls itself free.

Reel 7 · Try it

Don't take my word for it.

Two small demos, running on Paper Loop's own Claude account. Make a worksheet on any school topic, or write an answer and watch it get marked.

Pick a question from a Paper Loop sample

Write the answer like a student would. Get it wrong on purpose if you like.

A photo of an answer you wrote on paper works too. Please don't use real student work.

What you type, draw, or photograph goes to Anthropic's API to be read, and this site keeps none of it. To limit the demos, the site counts them by a scrambled form of your internet address and wipes the counts every night. Cloudflare, which hosts the site and runs the bot check when there is one, keeps its own short-term server records.

Credits · Who built it

I taught 8th-grade science at a Title I school in East Oakland.

At Downtown Charter Academy, when the school couldn't find money in time for a science graphic novel, I didn't ask families to pay. I raised $800 from my own network so every 8th grader, about 80 of them, could own a copy and meet the author.16
